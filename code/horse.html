HORSE Cheat Sheet

Five fixed-limit games rotating each level: Hold'em · Omaha Hi-Lo · Razz · Seven-card Stud · stud Eight-or-better. You can't punt a stack in limit — the edge is starting-hand discipline and small edges across all five.
The whole game in one line: play tight starting hands, draw only to the nuts in the split games and only when live in the stud games, value-bet relentlessly, and bluff almost never. Lose nothing in your weak game (Razz), grind your edges in the rest.

How the betting works (fixed-limit)

Every game has two bet sizes — a small bet and a big bet (double it), e.g. $100/$200. You never choose your size; you bet and raise in fixed increments, capped at 4 bets per street (a bet + 3 raises).

Where the bet doubles

Flop gamesH, O: small bet preflop & flop · big bet turn & river. Use blinds (SB = ½ small bet, BB = 1 small bet).
Stud gamesR, S, E: small bet 3rd & 4th street · big bet 5th, 6th, 7th. Use antes + a bring-in (no blinds).
Bring-ineveryone antes, then one player posts a forced part-bet: the lowest up-card in Stud & Stud-8, the highest up-card in Razz. Others can "complete" to the full small bet.

Limit Hold'em, step by step ($100/$200)

  1. Blinds: SB $50, BB $100.
  2. Preflop: call $100, or raise to $200 (one small bet). Re-raise $300, cap $400.
  3. Flop: bets & raises are $100 (small bet).
  4. Turn & river: bets & raises jump to $200 (big bet).
  5. No sizing choices. The skill is how many bets you get in (raise to build/charge) and whether the price is right to call — pots run big and multiway, so you chase real draws and call down lighter than in no-limit.

H · Limit Hold'em

The one you've never played. Same shape as no-limit, but fixed bets make it a value & showdown game — you can't bet big to protect or bluff, so you raise to charge and you call down.

Open withAny seat: AA–JJ, AK, AQs. Mid+: TT–99, AQ, AJs, KQs, ATs, KJs. Late / multiway: small pairs (set-mine), suited connectors, suited aces, suited Broadways. Fold offsuit junk & weak offsuit aces from early.
Chase / raiseRaise premiums for value & to charge draws. Chase flush/open-ender draws — limit pots give big odds, so 8–9-out draws usually have the price. Don't slowplay; you can't make it up with a big bet later, so get your bets in now.
BluffThin. Semi-bluff your draws (bet/raise with equity). Heads-up, a c-bet or turn barrel vs a tight player on a scary board works. Multiway or vs a station → don't; value-bet and call down.

O · Omaha Hi-Lo (8-or-better)

Four cards, use exactly 2 + 3. Pot splits between best high and best low (low = five unpaired cards 8-and-under; A-2-3-4-5 is the best low; the ace plays both ways; a low needs three low ranks on board to exist).

Bettinglike Hold'em — blinds, small bet pre/flop, big bet turn/river.
Open withA-2 + high backup: A-2-3-4, A-A-2-x, A-2 double-suited, suited aces. Coordinated high hands (A-K-Q-J ds). Premium = A-2-3-4 double-suited. Cards that work together.
Chase / raiseIt's a nut & scoop game: draw to the nut low (A-2) and/or nut high, raise hands that can win both. Never chase a non-nut low — you get quartered (split the low, win ¼).
BluffRare — multiway, split-pot, everyone draws to the nuts. Mostly value. Only bluff heads-up repping the nut low/high they can't have.

R · Razz — lowest hand wins

Seven-card stud, but the lowest hand wins. Straights & flushes don't count (ignore them), aces are low, pairs hurt you. The nuts is A-2-3-4-5 (the wheel, a five-low) — five unpaired low cards.

Bettingantes + bring-in (highest up-card brings in); small bet 3rd/4th, big bet 5th+.
Open withThree cards 8-or-lower, no pair — ideally three to a wheel (A-2-3, 2-4-5). Lower & smoother is better. A high card or a pair in your three → fold.
Chase / raiseIt's a footrace, read by up-cards: low showing = strong, high showing = weak. Raise when you hold the lowest board / they brick. Chase a live low draw in position. Give up when you pair or catch a big card and they catch low.
BluffBoard representation is the main bluff. You catch low while they catch high → bet/raise; they fold their bricked hand even if your real hand is behind. Steal the bring-in with a low up-card when folded to.

S · Seven-Card Stud (high)

Highest hand wins, normal rankings. 2 down + 1 up (3rd), 4th–6th up, 7th down. The skill is starting hands + live cards — track what's dead in opponents' up-cards.

Bettingantes + bring-in (lowest up-card brings in); small bet 3rd/4th, big bet 5th+ (an open pair on 4th can go big).
Open withRolled-up trips (3rd-street set — a monster), big pairs (especially hidden), three big cards, a three-flush or three-straight if live.
Chase / raiseRaise big pairs & made hands for value. Chase flush/straight draws only if live — if your outs are showing in others' hands (dead), the draw's worthless. Read the up-cards every street.
BluffRepresent a scary board — pair your door card (looks like trips), or show a 3-flush/3-straight. Steal antes with a high up-card. Bluff when your board out-scares theirs and they show weakness.

E · Stud Eight-or-Better (Stud Hi-Lo)

Your strongest game. Seven-card stud, hi/lo split (low = 8-or-better, same as Omaha 8). Scoop-oriented.

Bettingantes + bring-in (lowest up-card brings in); small bet 3rd/4th, big bet 5th+.
Open withThree low cards — best with an ace and suited/connected. A-2-3 is premium (nut-low draw + straight/flush + scoop). Big pairs for the high, cautiously.
Chase / raiseDraw to the nut low + a high and aim to scoop; raise the hands that can win both. Lows must be live (low cards not all dead in up-cards).
BluffBoard-rep like Stud, but the split pot shrinks bluffing — mostly value/scoop. Your existing Stud-8 notes apply here.

Chase · raise · bluff — across all five

The three decisions, unified — because the limit format makes them play the same way in every game.

CHASE

Limit = big pot odds, so real draws chase profitably — but only to the nuts in the split games (O, E) and only when live in the stud games (R, S, E). Count outs, compare to the price; for a true 8+-out draw you'll usually have it.

RAISE

For value — charge draws, build the pot, thin the field — and as a semi-bluff with a live draw. Raising in position can also buy a free card (raise flop, check back turn). Don't slowplay: limit can't recover streets you give away.

BLUFF — the short list

Bluffing is weak everywhere in limit — one more bet is a cheap call, and recreational fields + multiway pots call you down. The only bluffs that work: (1) semi-bluffs (you have outs), (2) board representation heads-up in the stud games (your up-cards tell a scary story — a bricked Razz board, a paired door in Stud), (3) ante / bring-in steals with a strong-looking up-card. The rule: bluff with equity, or with a board, heads-up — never naked, never multiway, never into a station.

Fixed-limit study aid for a soft mixed field — tight starting hands and nut-drawing beat this lineup by themselves. The discipline that wins: lose almost nothing in Razz, draw to the nuts in the split games, value-bet the rest, and let the recreational players spew the bluffs you're not making.
