The course: from rules to regular

Nine chapters in the order a hand is played, about five minutes each. Each one ends with the drill that practices it.

Opening: seats, sizes and limpers, Pro First in, over limpers and from the blinds: the ranges and raise sizes that hold up at a live 1/2 table where half the room limps. Read → Facing a raise: call, re-raise or fold, Pro Someone opened. Why their seat matters more than your cards, when a small pair can call to hit a set, and how the blinds defend against live-size raises. Read → Re-raised pots: 3-bets and 4-bets, Pro Build a 3-bet range that gets paid, size it to punish callers, and know what to do when you’re re-raised. At these stakes a 4-bet is almost always what it looks like. Read → Ranges: what they could have, Pro Stop asking what they have; ask what they could have. Counting combinations, shrinking a range with every action, and telling whose flop it is. Read → Betting the flop: board and crowd, Pro As the preflop raiser: when to keep betting (c-bet), when to check, and how big. The three sizing questions on the flops you actually see, heads-up and multiway. Read → Facing a bet: call, raise or fold, Pro The price says when a call is allowed; your hand against their betting range says whether it’s right. When to raise instead, when to check-raise, and when to lead. Read → Turn and river: betting for value, Pro Which turn cards keep your bets going, how thin to value-bet players who call too much, when a big river bet means what it says, and how deep one pair can go. Read → Beating the live field, Pro Type every opponent in two orbits, then change one thing against each: the nit, the station, the solid regular and the maniac. Plus the defaults that win in a loose, passive game. Read → Results, swings and the review habit, Pro How long before your results mean anything, what bankroll a 1/2 game asks for, and a weekly review that turns logged hands into better decisions. About

Pocket Tells

Log the hands that mattered in seconds, then drill the spots you got wrong. Everything stays on this phone: no account, no server, no analytics.

What the drill answers are. Good practice answers, not perfect ones; close calls are marked close.

Technically: preflop push/fold comes from an approximate-Nash solve; postflop is a heuristic engine calibrated by simulation. Both are checked against brute-force equity, and spots near a decision boundary are shown as a mix rather than a right answer. It’s a practice tool, not a full postflop solver. If a spot really matters, check it in one.
